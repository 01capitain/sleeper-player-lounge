# Research prompt — player background and personality material

Hand this to a research agent with web access. It produces the raw material for
[issue #1](https://github.com/01capitain/sleeper-player-lounge/issues/1); it does **not** edit
`data/players/star-players.json` directly.

---

## The prompt

> You are researching NFL players so a **fictional parody group chat** can reference them
> believably. The product is Players Lounge: drafted players react to a fantasy-football draft
> in a group chat. Dialogue is generated by an LLM that may only use facts we hand it, so your
> output becomes the entire raw material for a player's voice.
>
> Research the players listed at the end. For each one, find **specific, referenceable,
> football-facing material** — the kind of thing teammates would actually rib someone about.
>
> ### What makes a fact useful here
>
> Specific beats general, every time. "Competitive" is useless; "publicly listed every team
> that passed on him and still brings it up" is a joke generator. Prefer:
>
> - **Signature moments** — a famous play, catch, run, celebration, or gaffe
> - **Nicknames** and where they came from
> - **Celebrations, rituals, superstitions** — anything visible on a Sunday
> - **Draft-day story** — where he went, who passed, whether he felt slighted
> - **College and hometown** identity, if he leans on it publicly
> - **Rivalries and friendships** with other NFL players, especially at his own position
> - **Verbal tics and media persona** — how he actually talks in interviews, catchphrases,
>   whether he is expansive or gives nothing
> - **Running narratives** fans and media attach to him: injury-prone, underrated, overdrafted,
>   perennial breakout candidate, always traded
> - **Position-room dynamics** — who he shares targets or carries with, and any friction
> - **Self-aware material** — things he jokes about himself, which is the safest comedy source
>
> ### What to exclude — this is a hard boundary
>
> Do **not** collect: private or family life, relationships, medical or mental-health detail,
> legal matters, arrests, allegations, contract disputes framed as grievance, political or
> religious views, anything about minors, anything a reasonable person would find humiliating,
> or unverified rumour and speculation.
>
> The test: *would this player laugh at it in a locker room?* If not, leave it out. When a
> player's public story is dominated by something in the excluded list, return fewer facts —
> do not fill the quota with borderline material. **Fewer, cleaner facts is the correct
> outcome, not a failure.**
>
> ### Sourcing rules
>
> - Every fact needs a **source URL** and the **date you accessed it**.
> - Prefer primary and reputable secondary sources: NFL.com, team official sites, ESPN, The
>   Athletic, AP, major beat reporters. Wikipedia is acceptable as a pointer but cite the
>   underlying source where you can.
> - If you cannot source it, drop it. Do not infer, do not reconstruct from memory, do not
>   round a half-remembered story into a confident one.
> - Mark anything you are less than certain about with `"confidence": "low"` rather than
>   omitting the caveat.
>
> ### Output format
>
> Return **one JSON document** and nothing else. No commentary before or after.
>
> ```json
> {
>   "generatedAt": "2026-08-28",
>   "players": [
>     {
>       "name": "Travis Kelce",
>       "sleeperPlayerId": "1466",
>       "position": "TE",
>       "nflTeam": "KC",
>       "voice": ["outgoing", "pop-culture-heavy", "loudest person in the room"],
>       "speechPattern": "Long, riffing sentences. Starts stories mid-thought. Uses nicknames for everyone.",
>       "facts": [
>         {
>           "fact": "Won the 2013 'Catching Kelce' reality dating show before establishing himself as a starter.",
>           "angle": "Self-aware early-career punchline he references himself.",
>           "confidence": "high",
>           "source": "https://example.com/article",
>           "accessed": "2026-08-28"
>         }
>       ],
>       "hooks": [
>         "frames draft events as eras, tours and album cycles",
>         "teases other tight ends about being second in the pecking order"
>       ],
>       "rivalries": [
>         { "with": "Patrick Mahomes", "sleeperPlayerId": "4046", "nature": "teammate banter", "topics": ["targets", "who carries the offense"], "affinity": 0.9, "banter": 0.75 }
>       ],
>       "guardrails": [
>         "Never quote Taylor Swift lyrics — song and album titles only.",
>         "No claims about his private relationships."
>       ],
>       "notes": "Anything you judged borderline and excluded, and why."
>     }
>   ],
>   "excluded": [
>     { "name": "Player Name", "reason": "Public story is dominated by a legal matter; nothing usable within the boundary." }
>   ]
> }
> ```
>
> ### Field rules
>
> - `voice` — 3 to 5 adjectives or short phrases. How he *sounds*, not what he does.
> - `speechPattern` — one or two sentences on rhythm, length, punctuation habits. This is what
>   makes two players distinguishable when the name is hidden, so make it concrete.
> - `facts` — 4 to 8 per player. Each needs `angle`: how a group chat would actually use it.
>   A fact with no angle is trivia and should be dropped.
> - `hooks` — 3 to 5 recurring behaviours, phrased as instructions to a writer. These must work
>   on **any** pick, including picks that have nothing to do with this player — he is in the
>   chat all night, not only when he is drafted.
> - `rivalries` — real, sourced connections only. `affinity` and `banter` are 0–1.
> - `guardrails` — anything a writer must avoid for this specific player.
> - `sleeperPlayerId` — include when you are confident; otherwise omit rather than guess.
>
> ### Players to research
>
> *(list players here — start with the cast in `data/players/star-players.json`)*

## Why the format is shaped this way

`voice`, `hooks` and `guardrails` map directly onto the existing `star-players.json` schema, so
integration is a merge rather than a translation. `facts`, `speechPattern`, `rivalries` and
`excluded` are additions the research needs to be trustworthy and reviewable:

- **`angle` on every fact** is the important one. Real runs showed that specific hooks produce
  specific jokes and vague hooks produce filler — Kelce lands because his hooks literally name
  eras and tours, while a hook like "QB banter" produces exactly the generic line you would
  predict. Forcing an angle rejects trivia at collection time instead of at writing time.
- **`speechPattern`** exists because several current Regulars are distinguishable only by
  position. The success test in issue #1 is whether you can identify a speaker from the text
  alone with the name removed, and register is what makes that possible.
- **`hooks` must work on unrelated picks.** The cast is ambient — Regulars comment on picks that
  have nothing to do with them, weighted by `activity`. A hook that only fires when the player
  is drafted makes him silent for the other 237 picks.
- **`excluded` is a first-class output.** It shows the boundary was applied deliberately rather
  than forgotten, and stops a reviewer wondering whether a thin entry was laziness or judgment.
- **Per-fact sourcing** matters because these are real people. Anything unsourced is a
  fabrication risk with a real person's name attached.
